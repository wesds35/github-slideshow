# Lateral Knee Injury — Probability Analysis & 12-Week Criteria-Based Rehab Plan

**Prepared:** August 14, 2026
**Companion to:** Lateral Knee Injury — Context Document (Aug 14, 2026)
**Athlete:** Brown belt BJJ competitor, Masters 1. Leg-lock-centric game (SLX → ankle lock).
**Competition:** IBJJF Masters Worlds, September 2026 (~4 weeks out).

> This document organizes probabilities and a rehabilitation framework ahead of imaging and physician review. It is not a diagnosis and does not substitute for clinical evaluation. **No phase of this plan should begin before the plain film and PLC-protocol MRI described in the context document are obtained and reviewed.**

---

## Part I — Probability Analysis

### Method

Probabilities below are subjective clinical estimates conditioned on three anchors from the context document:

1. **Mechanism** — tibial external rotation in deep flexion (>120°), femur fixed, lateral pop mid-arc.
2. **Exam gradient** — prone 90° flexion functions well; heel drive (flexion + hip extension + rotation) notably worse; lateral cord present but softer than the uninvolved side; peroneal dorsiflexion intact; no extension block, no locking.
3. **Anatomy** — the fibular head is a shared insertion for LCL, biceps femoris (conjoined tendon), and popliteofibular ligament, so one insertional injury can mimic all three.

Two caveats govern everything that follows:

- **These are not mutually exclusive.** The lateral meniscus produces no hamstring findings, so a meniscal injury can coexist with any of the others. Probabilities for individual structures sum to more than the probability that exactly one thing is injured.
- **The stroke test is the single largest pending update.** The estimates below shift materially once effusion status is known (conditional table further down).

### Ranked estimates (pre-imaging, pre-stroke-test)

| # | Diagnosis | Estimate | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fibular head insertional injury** (partial biceps femoris / conjoined tendon strain, or nondisplaced styloid "arcuate" avulsion) | **~45–55%** | Best fit for every positive finding: lateral pop at the fibular head region, load- and position-dependent hamstring weakness (heel drive ≪ prone 90°), objectively softer lateral cord, intact peroneal nerve (argues against displaced fragment). The exam gradient specifically localizes to the insertion — a muscle-belly strain would degrade both tests, a neural cause would produce distal deficits. Within this bucket: partial tendon/conjoined-tendon strain ~35–40%, nondisplaced styloid avulsion ~10–15% (indistinguishable on exam; this is exactly what the plain film is for). |
| 2 | **Lateral meniscus, posterior horn or root** (including previously silent discoid meniscus) | **~30–40%** | The mechanism is the textbook one: deep flexion + tibial external rotation, with the lateral posterior horn translating ~10–11 mm posteriorly and getting pinched between condyle and plateau. A mid-arc pop fits a structure catching/subluxing better than a ligament failing at end range. Absence of locking/extension block rules out only displaced bucket-handle patterns — most posterior horn and root tears are mechanically silent. Produces no hamstring findings, so this estimate is largely **independent of #1** and the two can coexist. The stroke test is the discriminator. |
| 3 | **Popliteofibular ligament / popliteus** (partial) | **~15–20%** | External rotation is the vector these resist, and the PFL shares the fibular attachment — so a fibular-head injury (#1) plausibly involves it. Partially protected in this case because past ~90° flexion the PCL takes over as primary external-rotation restraint. A partial injury here produces a soft endpoint without frank laxity, which is precisely where physical exam sensitivity is poor — hence the PLC-protocol MRI. Note: because the biceps femoris is itself a superficial-layer PLC structure, **any confirmed #1 already places this case in the PLC category**; this row estimates deeper-layer involvement specifically. |
| 4 | **Proximal tibiofibular joint subluxation** | **~5–10%** | Kneeling with the foot tucked under is the classic mechanism, and PTFJ injury is routinely mistaken for LCL sprain (both are tender over the fibular head). But the mid-arc pop fits less well, and PTFJ subluxation does not explain the load-dependent hamstring weakness. The pending A-P translation test at 90° is the discriminator. |
| 5 | **Isolated LCL sprain** (the original working diagnosis) | **~3–5%** | Biomechanically the weakest candidate. The LCL is maximally taut in extension and essentially unloaded past ~70–90° of flexion; this injury occurred beyond 120°, in the exact position where the LCL is protected. Nothing in the mechanism preferentially loads it, and base rates agree — ≤25% of lateral-sided knee injuries are truly isolated LCL. The original 12-week LCL program was built on the least likely branch of the tree. |

**Ruled out** (per exam): complete distal biceps femoris rupture (cord palpable), displaced bucket-handle tear (no extension block), gross peroneal nerve palsy (dorsiflexion intact).

### How the pending stroke test shifts the table

| Stroke test result | Effect on estimates |
|---|---|
| **Any effusion** | Intra-articular process confirmed. Lateral meniscus rises to **~55–70%**; popliteus (intra-articular portion) rises; **PTFJ and isolated LCL drop to near zero**. Fibular head insertional injury remains possible as a coexisting extra-articular finding. |
| **Effusion within 2 h of injury (historical)** | Hemarthrosis — red-zone peripheral meniscal tear, cruciate, or osteochondral injury. Escalates urgency of MRI. |
| **Effusion at 12–36 h (historical)** | Reactive — avascular-zone meniscal tear or capsular irritation. |
| **No effusion, ever** | Extra-articular process. Fibular head insertion rises toward **~60–70%**; meniscus falls substantially (small tears can still be dry, so not to zero); PTFJ stays live. |

### Other pending discriminators

- **Figure-4 palpation, three points ~2 cm apart:** fibular head (+ A-P translation) → PTFJ/insertional; boggy or discontinuous LCL midsubstance cord → LCL; lateral joint line + positive Thessaly at 20° → meniscus.
- **Resisted eversion + first-webspace sensation:** completes the peroneal screen; any deficit escalates.
- **Plain film this week:** styloid avulsion is invisible on exam, visible on film, and converts this to a surgical conversation.
- **PLC-protocol MRI:** the only way to separate pop + ER mechanism + insertional weakness, which clinical exam cannot.

## Part II — 12-Week Criteria-Based Rehabilitation Plan

### Design principles

1. **Diagnosis-agnostic where possible, gated where it matters.** The plan below runs as written for the most probable diagnosis (partial fibular head insertional injury, grade I–II). Imaging results at Gate 0 either confirm that track or divert to a branch (Section "Branch points").
2. **Criteria-based, not calendar-based.** Weeks are targets, not entitlements. Every phase has entry and exit criteria; failing a criterion holds the phase, it doesn't skip it.
3. **The provocative vector is loaded last.** Tibial external rotation under deep flexion — the injury mechanism and the athlete's own A-game (ashi garami) — is the final thing reintroduced, not an intermediate step.
4. **Rules of thumb throughout:** pain ≤2/10 during work, no pain increase the following morning, no new or increased effusion within 24 h of any session. Violating any of these means repeat the last tolerated stage.

### Gate 0 (Week 0 — this week): imaging and triage

**Do before anything else:**
- Plain radiograph (rule styloid/arcuate avulsion in or out).
- PLC-protocol MRI.
- Complete outstanding exam items: stroke test, effusion-timing history, resisted eversion, first-webspace sensation, figure-4 three-point palpation, PTFJ A-P translation.
- Apply the Section 10 clearance criteria from the context document (varus stress 0°/30°, dial test 30°/90°, alignment, nerve screen).

**Interim restrictions remain in force** (no resisted knee flexion under load, no heel drives, no resisted or passive tibial ER, no deep flexion under load, no bottom-position grappling or leg entanglements). Permitted: bilateral sagittal-plane lower body (leg press, box squat to parallel), stationary bike, hip abductor work, full upper body.

### Branch points (imaging outcomes that divert the plan)

| Imaging finding | Action | This plan… |
|---|---|---|
| Fibular styloid / arcuate avulsion on film | Surgical referral. Acute PLC repair outperforms delayed reconstruction and that advantage decays after **2–3 weeks from injury** — the clock is already running. | **Void.** |
| PLC injury grade ≥ II (PFL, popliteus, or combined) on MRI | Surgical consult inside the same 2–3-week repair window. | **Void.** |
| Lateral meniscus **root** tear | Surgical referral — untreated root tear ≈ functional meniscectomy; repair window matters. | **Void.** |
| Lateral meniscus posterior horn tear, stable, non-root | Physician decision: conservative vs. repair. If conservative: run this plan **with the meniscal modifier** (no loaded flexion >90° until Phase 4; no combined flexion + rotation until Phase 5). | Runs, modified. |
| PTFJ instability | Different problem: proprioceptive/stabilization program, possible taping/bracing; physician-directed. | **Void** (replaced). |
| Partial insertional strain, grade I–II, PLC deep layer intact, cruciates intact | Proceed. | **Runs as written.** |
| Dial test >10° asymmetry, varus laxity at 0°, or soft endpoint at 30° at any point | Stop; surgical referral regardless of MRI read. | **Void.** |

### Phase 1 — Protection & baseline (Weeks 1–2)

**Goal:** calm the insertion down, keep the rest of the athlete trained, establish objective baselines.

- **ROM:** pain-free active knee flexion/extension, unloaded (seated or supine heel slides). No forced deep flexion. Full extension maintained daily.
- **Isometrics (only if pain ≤2/10):** submaximal hamstring isometrics at 30° and 60° flexion, prone or seated, 5 × 30–45 s, building toward 70% effort by end of week 2. Isometrics are analgesic and maintain tendon load tolerance without excursion.
- **Unaffected patterns:** leg press and box squat to parallel (bilateral, sagittal only), calf raises, hip abduction/adduction, glute bridges (bilateral, feet close, no heel drag), stationary bike with seat high and low resistance.
- **Upper body & core:** unrestricted, excluding positions that load the knee in flexion + rotation (no kneeling-based pressing).
- **BJJ:** none on the mat. Film study, instructionals.

**Exit criteria → Phase 2:**
- Imaging reviewed and cleared for the conservative track (Gate 0 passed).
- Pain-free full active ROM 0° to ≥120° unloaded.
- Hamstring isometric at 60°: ≥70% effort, pain ≤2/10, no next-morning flare.
- No effusion on stroke test.

### Phase 2 — Progressive concentric loading (Weeks 3–5)

**Goal:** restore hamstring strength through range, concentric before eccentric, short lever before long lever. Still **no combined flexion + external rotation**.

- **Hamstrings:** prone curls (machine or band), light, 3 × 12–15 → progress load weekly if morning-after rule holds. Double-leg bridge → feet-elevated bridge → single-leg bridge (short lever: heel close to hips loads the insertion less at long muscle length — start there, lengthen the lever across the phase).
- **Compound:** continue leg press/box squat, progress load; add step-ups (low box), RDL pattern with hinge kept above 45° trunk lean initially (limits long-length hamstring demand), kettlebell deadlift.
- **Rotation control (isometric only):** seated tibial-rotation holds against manual/band resistance at 90° flexion, submaximal — teaching the leg to resist ER, not produce it.
- **Proprioception:** single-leg stance progressions, unstable surface, ball toss.
- **Conditioning:** bike unrestricted; introduce rower **only if** the catch position (deep flexion under load) is pain-free at bodyweight first.
- **BJJ:** solo movement drills, sagittal only — shrimping, bridging, technical stand-up on the uninvolved side. No partner work.

**Week 4 checkpoint — the Worlds decision.** See "Competition decision" below. This is where the September call gets made, on imaging plus objective criteria, not on how the knee feels that week.

**Exit criteria → Phase 3:**
- Prone curl strength ≥70% of uninvolved side (dynamometer or 8RM comparison).
- Single-leg bridge, long lever: 15 reps pain-free, no next-morning flare.
- Isometric ER resistance at 90°: pain-free at moderate effort.
- Still no effusion.

### Phase 3 — Eccentric & early rotational loading (Weeks 6–8)

**Goal:** rebuild the insertion's tolerance for eccentric, lengthened-state load — the thing heel drive tests and the thing grappling demands.

- **Eccentrics (the core of the phase):** slider leg curls (eccentric-only → full cycle), Nordic regressions (band-assisted, partial range → progress depth before progressing to unassisted), single-leg RDL with progressively longer lever and fuller hinge.
- **Heel-drive rebuild:** reintroduce the heel-drive pattern itself — supine on a low box, hip extension + knee flexion combined — bodyweight, slow tempo, then load. This is the failed test from the original exam; it becomes the tracked metric.
- **Rotation:** active tibial rotation drills at 90° open-chain, then low-load resisted ER/IR through partial range. Deep-flexion rotation still off-limits.
- **Flexion depth:** goblet squat progressively below parallel if pain-free; loaded flexion >120° waits for Phase 4.
- **Plyometric entry (late phase):** bilateral pogo hops → low box landings, sagittal only.
- **BJJ:** partner drilling, **top position only**, cooperative pace. Passing, pinning, transitions. No guard sitting, no entanglements, partner instructed accordingly.

**Exit criteria → Phase 4:**
- Nordic (assisted, consistent depth) pain-free with symmetric break-point.
- Heel-drive test: subjectively symmetric, pain ≤2/10 under manual resistance.
- Single-leg RDL, full hinge, ≥ bodyweight-appropriate load, symmetric.
- Hamstring strength ≥85% of uninvolved side.

### Phase 4 — Sport-specific loading (Weeks 9–10)

**Goal:** deep flexion under load, reactive strength, controlled reintroduction of the injury vector.

- **Deep flexion:** loaded past 120° — deep goblet squats, kneeling sit-backs (heels toward hips — the injury geometry, now progressive and controlled), Petersen/poliquin-style step work.
- **Combined vectors:** flexion + rotation under low load — controlled lunge with trunk rotation, cable-resisted rotation in split stance and half-kneeling.
- **Reactive:** single-leg hops (forward, lateral, rotational — in that order), deceleration and cutting drills.
- **BJJ:** positional sparring at graduated intensity, top and neutral. Guard play (bottom) reintroduced **without** leg entanglements. Escapes, retention, sweeps. Explicit agreement with partners: no one attacks the legs yet.

**Exit criteria → Phase 5:**
- Hamstring strength ≥90% uninvolved; single-leg hop battery ≥90% symmetry (distance and triple-hop).
- Kneeling sit-back to full depth, bodyweight, pain-free — i.e., the injury position itself is tolerated statically.
- Full-intensity positional rounds (non-entanglement) with no pain during, after, or next morning.

### Phase 5 — Return to full training (Weeks 11–12)

**Goal:** the last 10% — the athlete's own game.

- **Entanglement ladder, one rung per session minimum, no skipping:**
  1. Ashi garami positions statically, cooperative partner, no submissions either direction.
  2. Athlete attacks from SLX/ashi; partner defends but does not counter-attack the legs.
  3. Defensive rounds: partner attacks the athlete's legs at graduated intensity; athlete practices clearing and escaping — this is where the injured knee sees uncontrolled ER, so it comes after the athlete has re-learned offense.
  4. Full leg-lock sparring, both directions, trusted partners, then open.
- **Strength:** full posterior-chain program including Nordics and GHR; maintain 2×/week in-season pattern thereafter.
- **Return-to-competition criteria (all required):**
  - Strength and hop symmetry ≥90% sustained across two testing sessions a week apart.
  - Full-intensity leg-lock sparring, multiple sessions, no pain/effusion/morning-after symptoms.
  - Heel-drive under maximal manual resistance symmetric and pain-free.
  - No apprehension in bottom ashi against resisting opponents (patient-reported; apprehension predicts re-injury).

### Competition decision — Masters Worlds (~Week 4 of this plan)

The context document's framing stands: **this is an imaging decision, not a symptom decision.** Concretely, at the Week 4 checkpoint:

| Situation at Week 4 | Call |
|---|---|
| Imaging showed avulsion, PLC ≥ II, or root tear | Already withdrawn — surgical track. The 2–3-week acute-repair window closes long before September; competing would spend it. |
| Grade I insertional strain, Phase 2 exit criteria already met, no effusion at any point | Competing is **defensible but not clean** — the athlete would enter the ashi/entanglement environment at roughly Phase 2–3 tissue tolerance, ~7 weeks ahead of the plan's own return-to-competition criteria. If he competes, it should be with an explicit agreement with himself about pulling guard/entanglement exposure, and acceptance that a re-injury likely converts a conservative case to a surgical one. |
| Grade II strain, or Phase 2 criteria not met, or any effusion history | Withdraw. The plan's math doesn't reach competition-grade tissue by September, and his A-game is the single highest-risk position for this exact structure. |

One asymmetry worth stating plainly: **the downside is not symmetric.** Skipping Worlds costs one tournament in a Masters career. Competing on an unhealed fibular-head insertion or unrecognized root tear risks surgery, a 6–9-month reconstruction timeline, and cartilage that does not come back.

### Monitoring log (track every session, all 12 weeks)

- Pain during (0–10), pain next morning (0–10).
- Stroke test weekly (any effusion = hold phase, reassess).
- Prone curl / heel-drive strength vs. uninvolved side, weekly.
- Any new mechanical symptom (catching, giving-way, PTFJ clunk) = stop, reassess, re-image if persistent.

*Prepared as a planning framework ahead of clinical review. All gates, especially Gate 0 and the branch points, are physician decisions. If any red-flag finding emerges — new effusion, mechanical locking, peroneal symptoms (foot drop, first-webspace numbness, eversion weakness), or laxity on varus/dial testing — stop the program and escalate the same week.*
